# Jobs, leases and the schedule

- **Jobs.** Each step that waits on the network (fetch, an AI task, a wiki step, a Vault mirror write (not built), a slice of an export, a slice of a restore) is a row in `jobs` with its state, attempts and next attempt time. A Grok Bot send and wait is a step inside a fetch or AI-task job, not a job of its own. Both targets run a claimed row through `executeClaimed` in `src/core/jobs.ts`. On Cloudflare a Queues message carries the job id, with `max_batch_size: 1`, so one invocation runs one job; in the container an in-process loop picks due rows. The row, not the message, is the source of truth: a lost message, a discarded retry or a restart loses no work, because the sweep re-queues due rows.
- **One run per job.** A runner starts a job only by claiming its row (`claim` in `src/core/jobs.ts`), one `UPDATE ... RETURNING` that matches only a due row (`isDue`): a `queued` row whose next attempt time has come, or a `running` row whose claim expired. A second message for a finished job, or a sweep that finds it, claims nothing. An attempt whose runner died counts when the next runner takes over its expired claim, so a job that always kills its runner still reaches `gave-up`. A runner writes its result in one guarded batch (see Database and search in `database-and-search.md`) that checks the row is still `running` at the attempt count and the `attempt_generation` it claimed (`holding`, `src/core/job-rows.ts`), so a runner whose claim was taken over writes nothing, even when the takeover restarted the count at 0 because the Item's content moved. The fetch result, the Item's status and the job's `done` state commit in that one batch, so a crash never leaves a `done` job with a `processing` Item.
- **Log entries.** The batches that publish a Digest, create a Topic, store an overview, store picks and finish a Tag or Topic merge also append their Log entry (`docs/wiki.md`, The Log), so an entry is as atomic as the change: a stale publication, a lost claim or a duplicate delivery of a job that already committed appends nothing, and each run keeps its at-most-one-model-call rule.
- **Deadlines.** Each job runs under a deadline below the 15 minutes of wall time a Queues consumer gets (Workers docs, Limits): an AI call gets 10 minutes, a fetch 2 minutes, a Digest job 12 minutes (the 10 minutes of its AI call plus the 2 minutes of its one hop, each under its own signal), an export or restore slice 10 minutes. A fetch past its deadline is not retried (`attemptFetch` in `src/core/fetch-attempt.ts` turns the aborted signal into the `timeout` result): an Item with no title yet ends `failed` with `timeout`, and an Item that already has a title is not marked failed, because it keeps the fetch result `timeout` and its status follows its other open jobs (`settle` in `src/core/settle.ts`). Every other job kind except `export` fails its attempt at the deadline and its row retries it. An export slice has no abort signal, so its deadline only sets the length of its claim. The consumer sets `limits.cpu_ms` to 300,000, the Workers Paid maximum, because a Queues consumer gets 30 seconds of CPU by default (Workers docs, Limits).
- **The export and restore jobs.** The kinds `export` and `restore` are the only two with no Item: `item_id` is null, `address` is `exports/<id>` or `restores/<id>`, and `claimedRow` in `src/core/jobs.ts` refuses such a row that has an Item. Each run does one slice (a restore: one unit of its `check`, `tables`, `files`, `search`, `highlights`, `finish` or `undoing` phase) and returns one checkpoint, which the runner commits in a guarded batch together with the job's close and the next job of that kind. The batch holds only while this run still holds the claim and the export or restore is still `running` at the part, phase and cursor the slice read (`stillAt` in `src/core/restore-unit.ts`), so a cancelled export, a runner that lost its claim and a repeated slice write nothing. An export slice that loses also deletes the objects it wrote, whose names carry the claim's attempt number, so they are never a committed slice's objects. The deadline is 10 minutes (`EXPORT_SLICE_DEADLINE_MS`, `RESTORE_UNIT_DEADLINE_MS`), the retries are the 3 attempts every job gets, and the third failure ends the run `failed` with `gave-up`. A refusal in the restore `check` phase is not retried: the unit ends the restore `failed` with the refusal code and supersedes the parts in the same guarded batch. On the container a start puts a running `restore` job back to queued, and on the Worker the sweep takes over an expired claim. The sweep stage `exports` (`cleanExports` in `src/core/export-cleanup.ts`), after `search` and before `restores` and `files`, removes the objects of a `removing` export, of a `failed` export 12 minutes after its last update and of a `ready` export 24 hours after it finished, at most 5 a sweep, and then deletes every object under `exports/` whose export row is `expired`, because a run that a pause held past its claim can put objects after the removal and stop before it deletes them; the bound is the next sweep that runs this stage, and only `enqueue` and `restores` run while a Restore runs or has failed with `gave-up`. A release without a kind never claims its rows (`ci-releases-and-rollback.md`, Export version and Restore version).
- **Values chosen for fetch jobs.** The fetch deadline is 2 minutes (`FETCH_DEADLINE_MS`), except for the grok CLI route of an X link, which waits for a CLI slot for up to 3 minutes and then runs for up to 3 minutes (`docs/architecture/fetchers.md`, X), so the fetch kind's claim covers both. A claim lasts the claimed kind's deadline plus 2 minutes (`CLAIM_MARGIN_MS`), so a Digest claim lasts 14 minutes, under the 15 minutes a Queues consumer gets. The 12 minutes of a Digest job are the sum of the two network and model phases; the database reads and the publication batch around them take milliseconds and are not part of the bound. The runner keeps one record per job kind (`HANDLERS` in `src/core/job-handlers.ts`), so a new kind adds an entry and no branch. A job gets 3 attempts (`MAX_ATTEMPTS`); a failed attempt is retried after 30 seconds, and a second one after 2 minutes (`RETRY_DELAYS_MS`); after the third the Item is `failed` with `gave-up`. On Cloudflare the retry is a Queues message with `delaySeconds`. The tick sweeps at most once per 30 seconds (`TICK_SPACING_MS`), claimed in `scheduled_runs` so two ticks do not both sweep. A stored time more than one spacing ahead of the clock counts as old, because the clock moved back, so a step back of an hour on the container's host does not stop the sweeps for that hour. The sweep enqueues at most 100 due rows (`SWEEP_LIMIT`) in one `sendBatch` call, which takes up to 100 messages and one `delaySeconds` for the batch ([Queues docs, JavaScript APIs](https://developers.cloudflare.com/queues/configuration/javascript-apis/)). The container loop runs 4 jobs at once (`LOOP_WIDTH`), looks for due rows every 5 seconds and calls the tick every 60 seconds. `JobLoop` ignores a `start` that is not from `idle`, so a second `start` or a `start` after `stop` does nothing, and a `stop` that arrives while `start` still migrates data wins. A run that is still going after its claim expired is logged as `job-run-abandoned` with the job id and stops counting toward the 4, because the sweep or the next poll may then give the job to another run. The run is not cancelled, and its guarded write discards a late result. No real input keeps a run going past its claim by itself, but a frozen process does: on 2026-11-15, 4 runs that froze the container process (`SIGSTOP`) past the claim of a job and resumed it logged `job-run-abandoned`, the loop gave the job to a takeover run, and the frozen run's guarded write was discarded with `job-claim-lost`.
- **Waiting for a vendor.** A handler that must wait returns a deferred outcome (`DeferredOutcome` in `src/core/job-outcome.ts`): an absolute earliest time, a wait reason (`WAIT_REASONS` in `src/core/kinds.ts`) and whether the attempt counts. It never throws for a vendor's delay and never sleeps. `queueLater` (`src/core/job-finish.ts`) sets the job `queued` with `next_attempt_at` at that time and stores `jobs.wait_reason` in the guarded batch that checks the claim, so the due time and the Item-facing reason commit together or not at all. The queue message is only a wake-up: `claim` takes a queued row only when `next_attempt_at <= now`, so an early or duplicate delivery claims nothing and a restart keeps the stored time. A deferred outcome can also carry `writes`, extra statements that commit in the same guarded batch as the deferral. The Worker clamps the message delay to 86,400 seconds, the Queues maximum ([JavaScript APIs](https://developers.cloudflare.com/queues/configuration/javascript-apis/): "Must be an integer between 0 and 86400"), and the sweep delivers a row that is due later than that. A Digest stays queued behind a waiting fetch like behind any open fetch. A job that waits for the Grok Bot's answer defers the same way, with the reason `grok-bot`, until the task's deadline, and the result route makes it due at once when the answer arrives (AI tasks and Providers, Grok Bot). The fetch job of an X link is such a job, and a full in-flight cap defers it 60 seconds with the same reason.
- **Fetched Items and Digests.** `settledFetch` in `src/core/fetch-settle.ts` increments the content generation in the same guarded batch whenever a successful fetch replaces the content, and the new text may be none (a repository without a README, an Article that ends without text, a Link-only result), so a Digest that started on the old text fails its generation check instead of publishing over cleared text. A Paste alone leaves the generation alone. A supplied Reddit thread increments it in its own batch even when it carries no post text. The same batch drops a pending caller-sent Digest when the Item has no post text and no eligible comment to publish over (`digestAfterFetch` in `src/core/digest-job.ts`, the one place both the fetch job and a supplied thread go through). It then opens a Digest only when SQL finds a Digest source (`hasDigestSource`) and saved settings or a pending caller-sent Digest, and marks a kept Digest "not updated" when no Digest job opens (`keptNotUpdated` in `src/core/digest-source.ts`). Link-only Items open no Digest. `providerSet` in `src/core/provider-saved.ts` is true when a `settings` row exists or Grok Bot is chosen as the Provider, so a Grok Bot Instance with no `settings` row opens its Digests like any other. With a saved key that no longer opens, the fetch still opens a Digest job, which gives up as `secret-missing` before any Provider call. A Digest job that finds no usable input left (no text, an unreadable key, a linked page that gave no text) marks the Item failed when a Provider is saved, so it shows Try again, and marks it as not updated otherwise (`unusableInput`). The Digest's publishing guard checks the generation again. A moved generation discards the result, fails that AI task as `stale` and requeues the job under its held claim at the same attempt count, with the stale-input backoff below. One model call per run keeps the Digest job's 12 minutes below Queues' 15-minute consumer limit.
- **One Item status rule.** An Item stores only whether it failed: `items.reason` is null, or one of the failure reasons, and a non-null reason is the failure marker (`IS_FAILED` and `NOT_FAILED` in `src/core/item-status.ts`). No column holds `processing` or `ready`. `STATUS_VIEW_COLUMNS` derives the status a reader sees in the query: `failed` with its reason when the reason is set, otherwise `processing` while a fetch, Digest or X check job is open and `ready` when none is. A job that opens or closes writes nothing to the Item. Only `fetch`, `digest` and `x_verify` jobs (`ITEM_JOB_KINDS` in `src/core/kinds.ts`) count; a `topic_overview`, `related_pick` or weekly job leaves the status of the Item it hangs on alone.
- **The Reddit turn.** The container runs 4 jobs at once, and two fills side by side would double the request rate of the owner's Reddit account, so the account step of a fetch of a Reddit link is a queue in the database (`src/core/reddit-account-queue.ts` writes its turn columns). `takeTurn` is one conditional `UPDATE` that sets `busy_until` (the fill deadline plus `CLAIM_MARGIN_MS`), `busy_job` and `busy_attempt`. The attempt number grows by 1 each time a `running` row is claimed again, so a takeover run has a larger one. It succeeds only when the run still holds its claim, the account is `connected`, nobody holds the turn (or this job holds it), `limited_until` and a used-up window have passed, and `queue_head` is empty or not older than this job. `queue_head` is the oldest job that waits for the turn: a job that defers with `reddit-limit` writes the smaller of its id and the head in the same batch, and the head stays until that job takes the turn or ends, so the order is the order the links were saved even while the oldest job is being claimed and its wait reason is empty. A job that does not win defers with `reddit-limit`, counts no attempt and sends no request, not even oEmbed, for at most 5 minutes (`WAIT_CHECK_MS`) or until the limit or the window reset. A deferral releases the turn and sets the head, a close makes the oldest waiting job due and releases the turn when this job held it, and that job is enqueued after the commit, so a batch of 100 links makes about one check every 5 minutes for each waiting job and no poll. A run that loses its claim releases the turn only when `busy_job` and `busy_attempt` are still its own, so the older run of a taken-over job leaves the takeover run's turn alone. A job that defers becomes due at once when the turn is already free for it, because the holder may have released between its refusal and its deferral and no one would wake it. Closing or deleting the job of a fill that is running does not free the turn. The fill asks before each request, the first one and the `me.json` check included, whether its run still holds the turn (`holdsTurn`), stops at the first request it may not send, and writes no window to the account row once it lost the turn, so a second fill never starts while the first still sends. A restart expires the claim of every running job (`abandonRunning`), the fill that was running takes its turn back through `busy_job`, and a crash leaves `busy_until` to run out after at most the fill deadline plus the claim margin. An `expired` account and Disconnect make every waiting job due in one batch, and those jobs run on the archive path. Before a rollback to a release that does not know the wait word `reddit-limit`, the owner presses Disconnect or runs the statement in `ci-releases-and-rollback.md`, Reddit account version.
- **One job close.** `closeJob` in `src/core/job-close.ts` closes a job in one batch with the Digest it owes, and `finish` and `exhaust` in `src/core/job-finish.ts` call it. A Digest job also opens the Digest that a pending caller-sent Digest still owes when that Digest differs from the one the job claimed. A save that supplies content closes the Item's open `fetch` and `x_verify` jobs and deletes its `x_verifications` row through `closeItemJobs`, which takes no `holding` guard, so a running fetch loses its claim and writes nothing. Give-up and exhaustion use the same path, so the paste card's Added state and the New marker wait until both jobs end. A Digest failure never discards fetched content or an earlier Digest.
- **Owed Digests.** The sweep stage `digests` (`sweepDigests` in `src/core/digest-job.ts`) opens at most 20 Digest jobs a sweep, oldest Item first, for Items that have a Digest source, no stored Digest, no failure marker and no open Digest job, while Grok Bot is chosen as the Provider. Items end in a Digest or the failure marker, so the rule needs no memory and repairs Items an older release left without a job. A model Provider is left out on purpose: an OpenRouter Connect whose key is not active yet saves its row and opens no job (`activation-pending`), and a Save of a model Provider opens the jobs through `olderDigests`. The jobs are queued by the next sweep.
- **Digest refusal policy.** Wrong keys, missing models, `refused`, `not-configured`, `secret-missing`, and HTTP 400 or 422 give up immediately. `unreachable`, `timeout`, `provider-busy` and schema failures after successful HTTP responses retry after the runner's 30 seconds and 2 minutes. The scheduled sweep closes running AI tasks as `failed/timeout` through `expireAiTasks` in `src/core/ai-tasks.ts`: at its deadline only a task that no job owns and no answer has reached, and any other task 24 hours after its deadline (`mayExpire` in `src/core/bot-task-states.ts`), covering abandoned calls after a crash or takeover. Each failed call ends its own AI task; only the batch that stores a Digest marks its task `done`. The stored Provider and model come from that call's result, so a settings change during a call cannot change its provenance.
  - **Stale input.** Every ending that stores the failure marker from an attempt's own read of the Item (a refusal, an input over the cap, an unreadable file, an empty input) writes it under the same generation and pending-value guard as a publication. When the Item's content or caller-sent Digest changed while the attempt ran, the guard fails and the job requeues without storing the marker, so the next attempt reads the new input.
  - **Attempts belong to a generation.** Every claim of a Digest job stores the Item's generation in `jobs.attempt_generation` (null on a job claimed before the column existed, which keeps its attempts), and a claim that finds a different stored generation starts the count at 0, so content that moved since the last attempt, after a refresh, a supplied thread or a crashed claim, gets its own 3 attempts. A job that runs out of attempts closes through `exhaust` in `src/core/job-finish.ts`, whose batch commits only while the Item still has that generation (`inputCurrent` of the Digest handler). When the Item moved in between, the job goes back to `queued` with its attempts and the next claim starts the count again, so the failure marker is never written for content that had no attempt.
  - **A caller-sent Digest that arrives mid-attempt.** Every claim of a Digest job also stores the Item's caller-sent Digest in `jobs.attempt_pending` (null when the Item holds none and on a job claimed before the column existed). The closing batch opens a new Digest job while the Item holds a caller-sent Digest that differs from `attempt_pending`, because a Paste can send a Digest after the attempt read the Item without changing the generation, and only a Paste can send it again. The new job takes the caller path (`attemptCaller` in `src/core/digest-attempt.ts`), which calls no Provider and publishes the Digest that the Paste's schema already accepted. The exhaust batch stores the marker exactly when the closing batch opens no Digest job for a held Digest (`markAttemptsSpent` in `src/core/digest-source.ts`): when the Item holds no caller-sent Digest, holds one that the job claimed (the job failed three times on it, so a repeat would fail the same way), or has no Digest source left, which the Item's pending Digest takes away from a Reddit link post whose only source is its linked page. A Digest that arrived after the last claim stores no marker, because the job it opens publishes it. Every other failure that lasts, such as an unreachable Provider, still ends as `digestFailed` once the 3 attempts on one generation are spent, and the job does not reopen itself.
- **Digest state and retry.** Only the failure marker is stored; `pending` comes from an open Digest job, and `src/core/digest-source.ts` holds the marker's writers. `markDigest` takes `cleared` (a Digest was stored or a retry opened a job) or `attempt-failed` (a refusal, an input over the cap, an unreadable file or an empty input on the last attempt), and both act only while a Digest job is open. `keptNotUpdated` clears the marker, then sets it again when an earlier Digest is kept and its `when` holds. Either outcome can coexist with a stored Digest. The Item view also carries its retry action (`paste`, `digest`, or none), so the UI follows one server rule: `shownStatus` in `src/core/shown-status.ts` gives a ready Item with a failure marker `digest` when `hasDigestSource` holds for it and `paste` when it does not. `POST /api/items/{id}/digest` opens one job over existing text at attempt 0 and leaves an already-open Digest or a failed Item unchanged: the statements that clear the pending Digest and open the job both name `NOT_FAILED`, so an Item that fails between the read and the batch gets no job. It never fetches the Item's own link (a Reddit post's linked page is read again by the Digest job, Fetchers, One hop). Both the owner's session and agent tokens may use it. A refresh that stores new text without opening a Digest job marks an older Digest as failed in the same batch. `saveAiSettings` opens older non-failed Items with a Digest source but no Digest or open Digest job, and no failure marker unless the latest Digest task failed as `wrong-key` (`olderDigests` in `src/core/digest-job.ts`), through one `INSERT ... SELECT` in its save batch, which also sets those Items `processing`. This costs one model call per eligible Item, at the existing runner concurrency.
- **Container restart.** When the container loop starts it marks the claim of every `running` row as expired (`abandonRunning`), because the process that held those claims is gone. The loop then claims the rows again, counts the lost attempt and runs them, so an Item that was `processing` at the restart leaves `processing` without a new Paste. The two ways a container stops differ. A graceful stop (`SIGTERM` or `SIGINT`, as `podman stop` and `podman restart` send) stops the timers, aborts running CLI tasks and waits up to `STOP_WAIT_MS` (`HOP_DEADLINE_MS` plus 1 s, so 9 s: a fetch that was mid-hop when the stop began ends on its own 8 s deadline inside the wait) for running jobs, ticks and queue reads to finish before it closes the database. Those jobs finish in the stopping process and write their result, so the next start takes nothing over and counts no attempt; a stalled fetch ends as `timeout`, `attempts: 0`. A crash (`SIGKILL`, the out-of-memory killer, a lost host, or a job still running when the wait ends) closes nothing: the claim stays `running` until the next start runs `abandonRunning`, which counts the lost attempt. One container runs on a volume. A second container started on the same volume runs `abandonRunning` too, so it expires the claims of the first one's running jobs and takes them: the job runs in both, each attempt counts, and only the second may write, because the first holds attempt 0 and the row is at attempt 1 (`claimNextDue`, `abandonRunning` and `holding` on one `threadsift.db` through `openDatabase`, 2026-11-10: the first container's `holding` was false, the second's true). Nothing stops that start, and a SQLite file on one volume is not a supported way to share one library between two containers. Run on 2026-11-07 against a stalled fetch: `podman restart` returned after 8.7 to 9.3 s (3 runs) with `attempts: 0`; `STOP_WAIT_MS` set to 0 gave `attempts: 1`; `podman kill --signal KILL` then `podman start` gave `attempts: 1`, `state: done`.
- **Leases with a generation.** A lease is a row with a holder, an expiry and a generation number that grows on every claim. Its expiry is the job's deadline plus 2 minutes, so a job that runs to its deadline still holds it. A job that writes under a lease commits in a guarded batch (see Database and search in `database-and-search.md`) whose guard checks that its generation still holds the lease and that the record it read is still at the revision it read; otherwise nothing is written and the job retries on fresh inputs. Jobs that need two leases claim them in Topic id order. Accept of a Topic merge is the one that does: each unit claims both Topic leases in that order and releases them on every exit (`docs/proposals.md`, Accept, Lease exits).
- **One update at a time per Topic.** Each wiki step that writes a Topic page holds that Topic's lease while it reads, calls the executor and writes. This is the spec's rule against near-duplicate pages from parallel updates (`docs/spec.md`, The library wiki). The `related_pick` step holds none, because it writes only rows of its own Item and the open-job index runs one pick for an Item at a time. With Grok Bot, the call returns later through a callback, so the overview step holds the lease for the read and the send only. It frees the lease in the same guarded batch that records the deferral (`DeferredOutcome.writes`), and applying the result is its own step that takes the lease again; a busy lease defers `topic-busy` for 15 seconds with no attempt (see AI tasks and Providers in `ai-tasks-and-providers.md`, The Topic lease around the wait). Because the lease is free while the bot works, the Grok Bot step also checks under the lease whether another job's `topic_overview` task for the Topic is still open (`overviewAtBot` in `src/core/bot-task-states.ts`). When one is, the step frees the lease in the deferral's own batch and defers `topic-busy` the same way, so at most one overview task per Topic is at the bot. Each bot run costs minutes, and a second task for the Topic would be refused as `stale` when the first stored the page, then sent again. A job that waited reads the Topic again once the task is applied, and it sends only when Items the stored overview was not built from remain. Otherwise it closes with no send, in a write guarded on the Topic still having no uncovered Item beyond those whose stored Digest cannot be read and no removed Item, so an Item whose Digest lands between the read and the close sends the step again through the stale requeue.
- **The Topic lease.** `src/core/topic-lease.ts` is the one module every wiki step uses, the units of an accepted Topic merge included. `claimTopicLease` is one `INSERT ... ON CONFLICT DO UPDATE` on `topic_leases` that takes the row when it is free, expired or already the holder's, bumps `generation`, and returns `null` when another holder has it. `leaseHolds` is the SQL condition a step puts in its guard and in its stale check. `releaseTopicLease` sets the expiry to 0 for that Topic, holder and generation only, so a release that carries an old handle changes nothing once the same holder has claimed again. `recoverPrefixLeases` releases every lease whose holder starts with a prefix; the `exhausted` batch and the Grok Bot overview's release use it, because they have the job and not the handle. `TOPIC_LEASE_MS` is `AI_DEADLINE_MS` plus 2 minutes, the bound of a job claim. The holder of `topic_overview` is `topic_overview:<job id>:<attempt>:<stale requeues>` (`overviewHolder` in `src/core/topic-overview-run.ts`), so a requeue over fresh inputs claims under a new holder, and a second runner of the same job finds the first one's live lease under another holder and waits. A job that finds the lease taken returns `{ deferredUntil: now + 15 s, wait: "topic-busy", countsAttempt: false }`, so waiting uses no attempts. The job releases the lease in the same batch as its result, in the discard batch when the guard fails, in a `catch` when the attempt throws, and in the `exhausted` batch when the job gives up. Failure markers are written only by the named writes of `src/core/topic-overview-run.ts` (`docs/wiki.md`, Topic pages): a refusal stores its marker for the Topic of the lease the attempt held, and the give-up batch stores `gave-up` for the Topic of the job's owner Item, or, for a job that sent a bot task, for the Topic named in the latest task's context (skipped when that Topic's page is newer than the task). The give-up batch also releases every lease whose holder starts with `topic_overview:<job id>:`, and the colon after the id keeps job 7 from releasing job 70's lease. The prefix, not the holder of the last attempt, finds the lease of a runner that died, because the job gives up at its claim with an attempt count that no runner held. A runner that dies keeps the lease until it expires, the only case that costs anyone the 12 minutes. The next claim of its job finds that lease under another holder and waits for it too, with no attempt counted.
- **New Topics and Tags.** A Digest job reads `catalog_version` with the Topic and Tag lists it gives the model. When its Digest reuses only existing names, it publishes at once, so an Import is not serialized. When it creates a Topic or a Tag, its publishing batch is guarded on `catalog_version` still being the version it read, and when the guard fails the job asks the model again with the new lists. `src/core/catalog.ts` uses `ON CONFLICT(key) DO NOTHING` and links by key in that same batch, so names another job created are reused. Each inserted name increases `catalog_version` once, and reused keys leave it alone. The guard also allows a moved version when every named key already exists. Only database errors consume an attempt at publish; lease loss and stale inputs do not. `jobs.stale_requeues` counts guarded stale requeues, including generation changes, up to `MAX_STALE_REQUEUES` in `src/core/job-outcome.ts`, and resets on an error retry or publish. `STALE_DELAYS_MS` makes the first two stale requeues due immediately and the third and later ones due 30 seconds later. After three stale requeues, a model result bypasses the catalog-version guard and publishes by key, accepting possible near-duplicate names to avoid starvation under catalog churn. A Grok Bot Digest skips the guard at its first answer (`digestBotKind` in `src/core/digest-bot.ts`), because every requeue sends a new bot task that costs a full bot run. A near-duplicate name can result, and the weekly Tag-merge and Topic-merge Proposals can suggest merging it, which the owner accepts or rejects (`docs/proposals.md`). The Item generation and pending-value guards still apply, so a refreshed Item cannot receive an obsolete Digest. Caller-sent Digests skip the catalog guard because no model can revise them. Every publication also carries a Pin guard on the Digest's Topic key and each of its Tag keys that no stale count lifts (Catalog keys, Pins). An accepted Tag or Topic merge bumps `catalog_version` once in its last unit, so a model Digest that read the old Tag list asks again while it has fewer than three stale requeues.
- **Catalog identity and linking.** `catalogKey` in `src/core/catalog-key.ts` writes the key described in Catalog keys, in JavaScript because SQLite lacks Unicode normalization and case folding. `linkOlderDigests` (`src/core/older-digests.ts`) links up to 10 unlinked stored Digests per sweep in Item id order with the same function, resolves each Digest's Topic and Tag names through the Pins, and guards its batch on the stored Digest and on that resolution. The singleton `catalog_linking.last_item_id` checkpoint advances past failed rows and wraps after the final page, so corrupt rows cannot starve later Items and are retried on later passes.
- **Caller-sent Digests.** `items.pending_digest` stores the last Paste's validated Digest, or null. The fetch drops it when it stores no text; otherwise the Digest job publishes it under the generation, pending-value and Pin guards, with every Tag name and the Topic name that a Pin covers written as the Pin's target, then clears the pending column. `startCallerTask` in `src/core/ai-tasks.ts` first creates its running AI task with executor `caller`, then publication completes it. A failed publish leaves a failed task row, as for a model result, and the sweep expires a stranded running row. Only a `running` task moves to `done` or `failed`, so a task the sweep expired stays failed. The publishing batch also commits only while its task is still `running` (`aiTaskRunning`), so a Digest never links to a failed task: the run counts as stale input and the job requeues with a new task. After the commit, `finish` looks up the Item's open jobs to wake the runner. When that query or the wake-up fails, the failure is logged as `jobs-not-queued` and the committed work and its task stay as they are, and the sweep finds the queued job. The publication keeps only the `keptComments` ids that are eligible comments of the generation it publishes, once each (`eligibleAmong` in `src/core/reddit-thread.ts`), so the stored Digest, the task result and the Kept flags name the same comments, as for a model result. A later Paste without a Digest and Try again use the model path.
- **The weekly claims.** The weekly Tag check and Topic check each claim their `scheduled_runs` row (`proposals`, `topic_proposals`) with `claimingWeek` (`src/core/scheduled-claim.ts`) and open one job in the same `db.batch` (`openModelRun` in `src/core/proposal-week.ts`). The job insert reads `changes() > 0` right after the claim, so two ticks that race open one job and a batch that fails spends no week. Due means no row, a `last_run_at` at least 7 days old, or one more than 7 days ahead of the clock (the clock moved back). The claim holds only while a Provider is saved (`providerSet`) and at least one Item has a Digest, so with no Provider nothing is claimed and the first check runs on the first tick after one is saved. The job is owned by the Item with the highest id that has a Digest, as a Topic overview is owned by an Item of its Topic, because every job kind except `export` and `restore` needs an owning Item (`ownedClaim` in `src/core/job-outcome.ts`; see Deleting an Item in `data-model-outline.md` for what deleting that Item costs). A job still open for that owner Item blocks the insert through the open-job index, so the claim is spent and no second job opens. The Topic claim has a row and a batch of its own, because the insert of the job decides with `changes() > 0` right after its own claim, and a second job insert after the Tag claim would read the first job insert's count (0 when the open-job index blocks it) and not its claim's. Two claims also make the weekly runs independent: a failing stage or job in one does not skip the other. Both jobs share the owner Item and have different kinds, so the open-job index allows both. The handlers have no `blockedBy` and take no Topic lease, because a Tag belongs to no Topic and the Topic check writes only `proposals` rows; the deadline is `AI_DEADLINE_MS`. The tick calls no model. A release that lacks `topic_proposals` leaves a queued job unclaimed, because `isDue` claims only the kinds in `JOB_KINDS` (CI, releases and rollback). When the stage throws before its batch commits, `sweep` logs `sweep-stage-failed` and the next tick tries again, because the claim did not commit. When the batch commits and its answer is lost (Guarded batches), the claim and the job are stored, the next tick finds the week claimed and opens nothing, and the sweep's first stage queues the stored job. When a job fails, a refusal of the saved settings or the address comes before any task row (`loadAiConfig` returns `taskId: null`), so the job leaves a `proposals-run-failed` or `topic-proposals-run-failed` line and no `ai_tasks` row; a refusal after the task was created leaves the line and the task row with its refusal. The page shows nothing about any of them, and its "last check" is the latest of the weekly claims, which does not say that a check finished. The two weekly checks that call no model (lost Highlights, Topics whose overview leaves out Items) and the disagreement check are in `docs/proposals.md`.
- **Schedule.** One tick, every minute, claims each due task in `scheduled_runs` and enqueues jobs: the three Instance-wide weekly model runs (a `proposals` job for Tags, a `topic_proposals` job for Topics and a `disagreement_proposals` job for Items; none is one job for each Topic or Tag the checks touched), the two weekly checks that call no model, which run in the sweep and open no job, and the sweep, which also continues every `accepting` Tag or Topic merge in its `merges` stage (`src/core/merge-accept.ts`; `docs/proposals.md`, Accept) and runs one page of the file cleanup (`cleanFiles` in `src/core/file-cleanup.ts`; see Files in `files.md`). The sweep (`src/core/sweep.ts`) runs its stages one after another, the re-queue of due jobs first, so the stages after it can fail without costing a lost Queue message or an expired claim its recovery. A stage that throws is logged as `sweep-stage-failed` with its name, the later stages still run, and the sweep then throws the first error, so the tick reports it (the local scheduled endpoint answers 500). The tick calls no model, because a Cron Trigger under an hourly interval gets 30 seconds of CPU (Workers docs, Limits). Cloudflare calls the tick from a Cron Trigger; the container calls it from a timer.
