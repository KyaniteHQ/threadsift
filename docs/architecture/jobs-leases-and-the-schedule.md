# Jobs, leases and the schedule

- **Jobs.** Each step that waits on the network (fetch, an AI task, a wiki step, a Vault mirror write, a Grok Bot webhook, a slice of an export or restore) is a row in `jobs` with its state, attempts and next attempt time. The same `runJob` code runs on both targets. On Cloudflare a Queues message carries the job id, with `max_batch_size: 1`, so one invocation runs one job; in the container an in-process loop picks due rows. The row, not the message, is the source of truth: a lost message, a discarded retry or a restart loses no work, because the sweep re-queues due rows.
- **One run per job.** A runner starts a job only by claiming its row (`claim` in `src/core/jobs.ts`): `UPDATE jobs SET state = 'running', claim_expires = now + deadline + 2 minutes, attempts = attempts + (state = 'running') WHERE id = ? AND ((state = 'queued' AND next_attempt_at <= now) OR (state = 'running' AND claim_expires < now)) RETURNING *`. A `done` row matches neither branch, so a second message for a finished job, or a sweep that finds it, claims nothing. An attempt whose runner died counts when the next runner takes over its expired claim, so a job that always kills its runner still reaches `gave-up`. The sweep enqueues every due row. A runner writes its result in one guarded batch (see Database and search in `database-and-search.md`) that checks the row is still `running` at the attempt count and the `attempt_generation` it claimed (`holding`, `src/core/job-rows.ts`), so a runner whose claim was taken over writes nothing, even when the takeover restarted the count at 0 because the Item's content moved. The fetch result, the Item's status and the job's `done` state commit in that one batch, so a crash never leaves a `done` job with a `processing` Item.
- **Log entries.** The batches that publish a Digest, create a Topic, store an overview, store picks and finish a Tag or Topic merge also append their Log entry (`docs/wiki.md`, The Log), so an entry is as atomic as the change: a stale publication, a lost claim or a duplicate delivery of a job that already committed appends nothing, and each run keeps its at-most-one-model-call rule.
- **Deadlines.** Each job runs under a deadline below the 15 minutes of wall time a Queues consumer gets (Workers docs, Limits): an AI call gets 10 minutes, a fetch 2 minutes, a Digest job 12 minutes (the 10 minutes of its AI call plus the 2 minutes of its one hop, each under its own signal), an export or restore slice 10 minutes. `fetchLink` builds a fetch job's deadline signal (`AbortSignal.timeout`) and passes it to `guardedFetch`, which combines it with each hop's 8 s signal through `AbortSignal.any` and turns an aborted job signal into the `timeout` result, so a fetch past its deadline ends the Item `failed` with `timeout` and is not retried. Every other job kind fails its attempt at the deadline and its row retries it. The consumer sets `limits.cpu_ms` to 300,000, the Workers Paid maximum, because a Queues consumer gets 30 seconds of CPU by default (Workers docs, Limits).
- **Values chosen for fetch jobs** (`src/core/jobs.ts` and `src/targets/node/job-loop.ts`). The deadline is 2 minutes (`FETCH_DEADLINE_MS` in `src/core/fetch-link.ts`) and a claim lasts the claimed kind's deadline plus 2 minutes (`CLAIM_MARGIN_MS`), so a Digest claim lasts 14 minutes, under the 15 minutes a Queues consumer gets. The 12 minutes are the sum of the two network and model phases; the database reads and the publication batch around them take milliseconds and are not part of the bound. The runner keeps one record per job kind (`HANDLERS` in `src/core/jobs.ts`: its deadline, how an attempt runs, and what giving up writes), so a new kind adds an entry and no branch. A job gets 3 attempts (`MAX_ATTEMPTS`); a failed attempt is retried after 30 seconds, and a second one after 2 minutes (`RETRY_DELAYS_MS`); after the third the Item is `failed` with `gave-up`. On Cloudflare the retry is a Queues message with `delaySeconds`. The tick sweeps at most once per 30 seconds (`TICK_SPACING_MS`), claimed in `scheduled_runs` so two ticks do not both sweep (a stored time more than one spacing ahead of the clock counts as old, because the clock moved back, so a step back of an hour on the container's host does not stop the sweeps for that hour), and enqueues at most 100 due rows (`SWEEP_LIMIT`) in one `sendBatch` call, which takes up to 100 messages and one `delaySeconds` for the batch (Queues docs, JavaScript APIs: https://developers.cloudflare.com/queues/configuration/javascript-apis/); the container wakes its loop once. The container loop runs 4 jobs at once (`LOOP_WIDTH`), looks for due rows every 5 seconds (`LOOP_POLL_MS`) and calls the tick every 60 seconds (`TICK_MS`). `JobLoop` keeps its life in one state (`idle`, `starting`, `running`, `stopping`, `stopped`): `start` does nothing unless the loop is `idle`, so a second `start` or a `start` after `stop` is ignored, and a `stop` that arrives while `start` still migrates data wins. A tick that finds the last one still running is skipped. A wake that arrives while the loop is looking for jobs always starts one more pass, even when that pass threw. The stop wait (`STOP_WAIT_MS`) races the jobs in flight against a timer that is cleared as soon as they settle. A run that is still going after its claim expired is logged as `job-run-abandoned` with the job id and stops counting toward the 4, because the sweep or the next poll may then give the job to another run; the run is not cancelled, and its guarded write discards a late result. The abandoned log is unverified: the claim of a run is its kind's deadline plus 2 minutes, and no real input keeps a run going that long, so no run has reached it.
- **Waiting for a vendor.** A handler that must wait returns a deferred outcome (`DeferredOutcome` in `src/core/job-outcome.ts`): an absolute earliest time, a wait reason (`WAIT_REASONS` in `src/core/kinds.ts`) and whether the attempt counts. It never throws for a vendor's delay and never sleeps. `applyOutcome` passes it to `queueLater` (`src/core/job-finish.ts`), which in the guarded batch that checks the claim sets the job `queued` with `next_attempt_at` at that time, clears the claim and stores `jobs.wait_reason`, so the due time and the Item-facing reason commit together or not at all; a runner whose claim was taken over writes nothing and logs `job-claim-lost`. `claim` clears `wait_reason`. The queue message is only a wake-up: `claim` takes a queued row only when `next_attempt_at <= now`, so an early or duplicate delivery claims nothing and a restart keeps the stored time. A deferred outcome can also carry `writes`, extra statements that commit in the same guarded batch as the deferral. The Worker clamps the message delay to 86,400 seconds, the Queues maximum ([JavaScript APIs](https://developers.cloudflare.com/queues/configuration/javascript-apis/): "Must be an integer between 0 and 86400"), and the sweep delivers a row that is due later than that. The Item answers read the waiting fetch job (`WAIT_VIEW_COLUMNS` in `src/core/joined-view.ts`) and carry `wait: { reason, retryAt }` while the Item is `processing`; the row, the paste card and the Reader show it, and say it is waiting to retry once `retryAt` has passed in the browser's clock (`web/src/WaitText.tsx`, with the text of each reason in `web/src/text.ts`). A Digest stays queued behind a waiting fetch like behind any open fetch. A job that waits for the Grok Bot's answer defers the same way, with the reason `grok-bot`, until the task's deadline, and the result route makes it due at once when the answer arrives (AI tasks and Providers, Grok Bot). The fetch job of an X link is such a job: it shows `grok-bot` while the bot reads the post, a full in-flight cap defers it 60 seconds with the same reason, and the intake route closes it when the answer arrives.
- **Fetched Items and Digests.** `src/core/fetch-job.ts` increments the content generation in the same guarded batch whenever a successful fetch replaces the content, and the new text may be none (a repository without a README, an Article that ends without text, a Link-only result): a Digest that started on the old text then fails its generation check instead of publishing over cleared text. The same batch first drops a pending caller-sent Digest when the Item has no post text and no eligible comment to publish over (`digestAfterFetch` in `src/core/digest-job.ts`, the one place both the fetch job and a supplied thread go through), then opens a Digest only when SQL finds a Digest source (`hasDigestSource`: post text, an eligible comment or, with no caller-sent Digest pending, a Reddit candidate link) and saved settings or a pending caller-sent Digest, and marks a kept Digest "not updated" when no Digest job opens (`keptNotUpdated` in `src/core/digest-source.ts`). A Digest job that finds no usable input left (no text, an unreadable key, a linked page that gave no text) marks the Item failed when a Provider is saved, so it shows Try again, and marks it as not updated otherwise (`unusableInput` in `src/core/digest-source.ts`). A Paste alone leaves the generation alone. A supplied Reddit thread increments the generation in its own batch even when it carries no post text, and a retained Digest whose text it cleared is marked as not updated (`src/core/thread-publish.ts`). Link-only Items open no Digest. `providerSet` in `src/core/provider-saved.ts` is true when a `settings` row exists or Grok Bot is chosen as the Provider (`grok_bot_settings.use_as_provider`), so a Grok Bot Instance with no `settings` row opens its Digests like any other. With a saved key that no longer opens, the fetch still opens a Digest job, which gives up as `secret-missing` before any Provider call. The runner applies each handler's `blockedBy` list before claiming a job, so a Digest stays queued behind an open fetch. `src/core/digest-attempt.ts` then calls the task's `prepareInput` on the fetched title, Source and post text (read from the file store when the row holds a key). Its publishing guard checks the generation again. A moved generation discards the result, fails that AI task as `stale` and requeues the job under its held claim at the same attempt count, with the stale-input backoff below. One model call per run keeps the Digest job's 12 minutes (10 for the model call, 2 for the hop) below Queues' 15-minute consumer limit.
- **One Item status rule.** `src/core/item-status.ts` owns an Item's status. A writer names an event (`StatusEvent`) and never spells `processing`, `ready` or a `case` over the status: `queued` (a fetch or Digest job is opening: `processing`, no reason), `settled` (content arrived or a fetch ended without failing the Item: the failure is cleared, then the open jobs decide), `failed` (a fetch gave up or failed with its reason) and `jobs-changed` (a job closed: a failed Item stays failed with its reason, otherwise any open fetch or Digest job makes it `processing` and none makes it `ready`). `statusColumns` turns an event into the columns to write and `setStatus` is the update statement. A row that is inserted with a status passes `NO_OPEN_JOBS`, because it has no job yet (`settledNew` in `src/core/settle.ts`); every other new Item takes the column default and the `queued` or `settled` event of its batch sets the status. A supplied thread (`threadPublication` in `src/core/thread-publish.ts`) ends with `settled` instead of `jobs-changed`, because a thread replaces a failed fetch. Only `fetch` and `digest` jobs (`ITEM_JOB_KINDS` in `src/core/kinds.ts`, the kinds `hasOpenJob` counts) own an Item's status; a `topic_overview`, `related_pick` or weekly job leaves the status of the Item it hangs on alone.
- **The Reddit turn.** The container runs 4 jobs at once, and two fills side by side would double the request rate of the owner's Reddit account, so the account step of a fetch of a Reddit link is a queue in the database (`src/core/reddit-account-states.ts`, the one writer of `reddit_account`). `takeTurn` is one conditional `UPDATE` that sets `busy_until` (the fill deadline plus `CLAIM_MARGIN_MS`) and `busy_job`, and succeeds only when the account is `connected`, nobody holds the turn (or the holder's job is closed or gone, or this job holds it), `limited_until` and a used-up window have passed, and `queue_head` is empty or not older than this job. `queue_head` is the oldest job that waits for the turn: a job that defers with `reddit-limit` writes the smaller of its id and the head in the same batch, and the head stays until that job takes the turn or ends, so the order is the order the links were saved even while the oldest job is being claimed and its wait reason is empty. A job that does not win defers with `reddit-limit`, counts no attempt and sends no request, not even oEmbed, for at most 5 minutes (`WAIT_CHECK_MS`) or until the limit or the window reset. The fetch handler owns the statements (`closing` and `afterClosed` on `JobHandler`, implemented only for a Reddit address, so a fetch of another source runs none): a deferral releases the turn and sets the head, a close makes the oldest waiting job due and releases the turn when this job held it, and that job is enqueued after the commit, so a batch of 100 links makes about one check every 5 minutes for each waiting job and no poll. A job that loses its claim releases the turn the same way. The queue lives in `jobs` and `reddit_account`: a restart expires the claim of every running job (`abandonRunning`), the fill that was running takes its turn back through `busy_job`, and a crash leaves `busy_until` to run out after at most the fill deadline plus the claim margin. An `expired` account and Disconnect make every waiting job due in one batch, and those jobs run on the archive path. Before a rollback to a release that does not know the wait word `reddit-limit`, the owner presses Disconnect or runs the statement in `ci-releases-and-rollback.md`, Reddit account version.

- **One job close.** `closeJob` in `src/core/job-close.ts` closes a job and settles its Item in the same batch, and `finish` and `exhaust` in `src/core/job-finish.ts` call it. A closed job is `done` with no claim, wait reason, attempt generation, stale requeues or page (`CLOSED_JOB` in the same file), and a Digest job also opens the Digest that a pending caller-sent Digest still owes. A save that supplies content closes the Item's open fetch jobs through `closeFetchJobs` in the same file, which takes no `holding` guard, so a running fetch loses its claim and writes nothing. That statement does not touch the Item; the `queued` or `settled` event later in the same batch writes its status once. Give-up and exhaustion use the same path, so the paste card's Added state and the New marker wait until both jobs end. A Digest failure never discards fetched content or an earlier Digest.
- **Shown status.** `items.status` stays written by every event for the previous release, which reads it (`docs/architecture/ci-releases-and-rollback.md`, Rollback). The Item view does not trust it: `STATUS_VIEW_COLUMNS` selects the same expression `jobs-changed` writes, so a failed Item shows `failed` with its reason, and any other Item shows `processing` while a fetch or Digest job is open and `ready` when none is, whatever the column holds. `shownStatus` in `src/core/shown-status.ts` adds the retry action, and `src/http/item-view.ts` maps both to the response. A row that contradicts its jobs is shown and read with 200, not dropped from the list or answered with 500. Dropping the column waits for the release after the one that reads only the jobs.
- **Owed Digests.** The sweep stage `digests` (`sweepDigests` in `src/core/digest-job.ts`) opens at most 20 Digest jobs a sweep, oldest Item first, for Items that have a Digest source, no stored Digest, no failure marker and no open Digest job, while Grok Bot is chosen as the Provider. Items end in a Digest or the failure marker, so the rule needs no memory and repairs Items an older release left without a job. A model Provider is left out on purpose: an OpenRouter Connect whose key is not active yet saves its row and opens no job (`activation-pending`), and a Save of a model Provider opens the jobs through `olderDigests`. The jobs are queued by the next sweep.
- **Digest refusal policy.** Wrong keys, missing models, `refused`, `not-configured`, `secret-missing`, and HTTP 400 or 422 give up immediately. `unreachable`, `timeout`, `provider-busy` and schema failures after successful HTTP responses retry after the runner's 30 seconds and 2 minutes. The scheduled sweep closes running AI tasks past their deadline as `failed/timeout` through `expireAiTasks` in `src/core/ai-tasks.ts`, covering abandoned calls after a crash or takeover. Each failed call ends its own AI task; only the batch that stores a Digest marks its task `done`. The stored Provider and model come from that call's result, so a settings change during a call cannot change its provenance. Every ending that stores the failure marker (a refusal, the third failed attempt, an input over the cap, an unreadable file) writes it under the same generation and pending-value guard as a publication. When the Item's content or caller-sent Digest changed while the attempt ran, the guard fails and the job requeues without storing the marker, so the next attempt reads the new input. A Digest job's attempts belong to the content generation it claimed them on: every claim of a Digest job stores the Item's generation in `jobs.attempt_generation` (migration `0030_attempt-generation-and-data-step-lease.sql`; null on a job claimed before the column existed, which keeps its attempts), and a claim that finds a different stored generation starts the count at 0, so content that moved since the last attempt, after a refresh, a supplied thread or a crashed claim, gets its own 3 attempts. A job that runs out of attempts on the generation it claimed closes through `exhaust` in `src/core/job-finish.ts`, whose batch commits only while the Item still has that generation (`inputCurrent` of the Digest handler); when the Item moved in between, the job goes back to `queued` with its attempts and the next claim starts the count again, so the failure marker is never written for content that had no attempt. The closing batch (`closeJob` in `src/core/job-close.ts`) also opens a new Digest job while the Item holds a caller-sent Digest, because only a Paste can send it again. Failures that last, such as an unreachable Provider, still end as `digestFailed` once the 3 attempts on one generation are spent, so a job does not reopen itself for the same content.
- **Digest state and retry.** The shared Digest projection in `src/core/joined-view.ts` and `src/core/item-selection.ts`, used by `src/core/items.ts`, carries the stored title and Topic apart from `digestOutcome`, the latest run's outcome. `pending` comes from an open Digest job; only the failure marker is stored, and `src/core/digest-source.ts` holds its writers. `markDigest` takes `cleared` (a Digest was stored or a retry opened a job) or `attempt-failed` (a refusal or the last attempt), and both act only while a Digest job is open. `keptNotUpdated` clears the marker, then sets it again when an earlier Digest is kept and its `when` holds. Either outcome can coexist with a stored Digest. The Item view also carries its retry action (`paste`, `digest`, or none), so the UI follows one server rule: `shownStatus` in `src/core/shown-status.ts` gives a ready Item with a failure marker `digest` when `hasDigestSource` holds for it and `paste` when it does not. `POST /api/items/{id}/digest` opens one job over existing text at attempt 0 and leaves an already-open Digest or a failed Item unchanged: `retryDigest` answers a failed Item before it writes, and the statements that clear the pending Digest, open the job and set the status all name `NOT_FAILED` in `src/core/item-status.ts`, so an Item that fails between the read and the batch gets no job and no status change; it never fetches the Item's own link (a Reddit post's linked page is read again by the Digest job, Fetchers, One hop). Both the owner's session and agent tokens may use it. `web/src/ItemRow.tsx` uses the Digest title and Topic, shows No Digest only for a failure without a stored Digest, shows Digest not updated when an older Digest is kept, and offers Try again for either failure state, in the row and, through `retryItem` in `web/src/use-list-actions.ts`, beside the Reader's Digest note and a failed Item's failure note in `web/src/ReaderColumn.tsx`, which pastes the link again for an Item with no Digest source. A refresh that stores new text without opening a Digest job marks an older Digest as failed in the same batch. `saveAiSettings` opens older non-failed Items with a Digest source but no Digest, failure marker or open Digest job through one `INSERT ... SELECT` in its save batch, which also sets those Items `processing`. The sweep and container loop start the jobs. This costs one model call per eligible Item, at the existing runner concurrency.
- **Container restart.** When the container loop starts it marks the claim of every `running` row as expired (`abandonRunning`), because the process that held those claims is gone. The loop then claims the rows again, counts the lost attempt and runs them, so an Item that was `processing` at the restart leaves `processing` without a new Paste. The two ways a container stops differ. A graceful stop (`SIGTERM` or `SIGINT`, as `podman stop` and `podman restart` send) stops the timers, aborts running CLI tasks and waits up to `STOP_WAIT_MS` (`HOP_DEADLINE_MS` plus 1 s, so 9 s: a fetch that was mid-hop when the stop began ends on its own 8 s deadline inside the wait) for running jobs, ticks and queue reads to finish before it closes the database. Those jobs finish in the stopping process and write their result, so the next start takes nothing over and counts no attempt; a stalled fetch ends as `timeout`, `attempts: 0`. A crash (`SIGKILL`, the out-of-memory killer, a lost host, or a job still running when the wait ends) closes nothing: the claim stays `running` until the next start runs `abandonRunning`, which is the takeover described above and counts the lost attempt. One container runs on a volume. A second container started on the same volume runs `abandonRunning` too, so it expires the claims of the first one's running jobs and takes them: the job runs in both, each attempt counts, and only the second may write, because the first holds attempt 0 and the row is at attempt 1 (`claimNextDue`, `abandonRunning` and `holding` on one `threadsift.db` through `openDatabase`, 2026-11-10: the first container's `holding` was false, the second's true). Nothing stops that start, and a SQLite file on one volume is not a supported way to share one library between two containers. Run on 2026-11-07 against a stalled fetch: `podman restart` returned after 8.7 to 9.3 s (3 runs) with `attempts: 0`; `STOP_WAIT_MS` set to 0 gave `attempts: 1`; `podman kill --signal KILL` then `podman start` gave `attempts: 1`, `state: done`.
- **Leases with a generation.** A lease is a row with a holder, an expiry and a generation number that grows on every claim. Its expiry is the job's deadline plus 2 minutes, so a job that runs to its deadline still holds it. A job that writes under a lease commits in a guarded batch (see Database and search in `database-and-search.md`) whose guard checks that its generation still holds the lease and that the record it read is still at the revision it read; otherwise nothing is written and the job retries on fresh inputs. Jobs that need two leases claim them in Topic id order. Accept of a Topic merge is the one that does: each unit claims both Topic leases in that order and releases them on every exit (`docs/proposals.md`, Accept, Lease exits).
- **One update at a time per Topic.** Each wiki step that writes a Topic page holds that Topic's lease while it reads, calls the executor and writes. The `related_pick` step holds none, because it writes only rows of its own Item and the open-job index runs one pick for an Item at a time. This is the spec's rule against near-duplicate pages from parallel updates (`docs/spec.md`, The library wiki). With Grok Bot, the call returns later through a callback, so the overview step holds the lease for the read and the send only. It frees the lease in the same guarded batch that records the deferral (`DeferredOutcome.writes`), and applying the result is its own step that takes the lease again; a busy lease defers `topic-busy` for 15 seconds with no attempt (see AI tasks and Providers in `ai-tasks-and-providers.md`, The Topic lease around the wait). Because the lease is free while the bot works, the step also checks under the lease whether another job's Grok Bot `topic_overview` task for the Topic is still open (`overviewAtBot` in `src/core/bot-task-states.ts`: running, and either answered or unanswered before its deadline; AI tasks and Providers, The Topic lease around the wait, says which rows count). Only the Grok Bot step runs the check. When a task is open, the step frees the lease in the deferral's own batch and defers `topic-busy` the same way, so at most one overview task per Topic is at the bot. Each bot run costs minutes, and a second task for the Topic would be refused as `stale` when the first stored the page, then sent again. A job that waited reads the Topic again once the task is applied, and it sends only when Items the stored overview was not built from remain. Otherwise it closes with no send, in a write guarded on the Topic still having no uncovered Item beyond those whose stored Digest cannot be read and no removed Item, so an Item whose Digest lands between the read and the close sends the step again through the stale requeue.
- **The Topic lease.** `src/core/topic-lease.ts` is the one module every wiki step uses, the units of an accepted Topic merge included. `claimTopicLease(db, topicId, holder)` is one `INSERT ... ON CONFLICT DO UPDATE` on `topic_leases` that takes the row when it is free, expired or already the holder's, bumps `generation`, and returns `null` when another holder has it. `leaseHolds(lease)` is the SQL condition a step puts in its guard (`insertGuard`) and in its stale check: the row still names that holder at that generation and has not expired or been released. `releaseTopicLease(db, lease)` is the batch item that sets the expiry to 0 for that Topic, holder and generation only, so a release that carries an old handle changes nothing once the same holder has claimed again (`generation` rises on every claim, including the same holder's). `releaseTopicLeases(db, leases)` batches the releases of several handles (a Topic merge unit holds two); `recoverPrefixLeases(db, holderPrefix)` releases every lease whose holder starts with the prefix; the `exhausted` batch and the Grok Bot overview's release use it, because they have the job and not the handle. `TOPIC_LEASE_MS` is `AI_DEADLINE_MS` plus 2 minutes, the bound of a job claim. `topic_overview` is the first user, with the holder `topic_overview:<job id>:<attempt>:<stale requeues>` (`overviewHolder` in `src/core/topic-overview-run.ts`), so a requeue over fresh inputs claims under a new holder, and a second runner of the same job finds the first one's live lease under another holder and waits. A job that finds the lease taken returns `{ deferredUntil: now + 15 s, wait: "topic-busy", countsAttempt: false }`, so waiting uses no attempts. The job releases the lease in the same batch as its result, in the discard batch when the guard fails (`currency` in `src/core/digest-source.ts` takes the release as an extra discard write), in a `catch` when the attempt throws, and in the `exhausted` batch when the job gives up. Failure markers are written only by the named writes of `src/core/topic-overview-run.ts` (`docs/wiki.md`, Topic pages): a refusal stores its marker for the Topic of the lease the attempt held (`overviewRefused`), and the give-up batch (`overviewGaveUp`) stores `gave-up` for the Topic of the job's owner Item, or, for a job that sent a bot task, for the Topic named in the latest task's context (skipped when that Topic's page is newer than the task, `markTopicOverviewFailed`), and releases every lease whose holder starts with `topic_overview:<job id>:` (`recoverPrefixLeases`; the colon after the id keeps job 7 from releasing job 70's lease). The prefix, not the holder of the last attempt, finds the lease of a runner that died, because the job gives up at its claim with an attempt count that no runner held. A runner that dies keeps the lease until it expires, the only case that costs anyone the 12 minutes. The next claim of its job finds that lease under another holder and waits for it too, with no attempt counted.
- **New Topics and Tags.** A Digest job reads `catalog_version` with the Topic and Tag lists it gives the model. When its Digest reuses only existing names, it publishes at once, so an Import is not serialized. When it creates a Topic or a Tag, its publishing batch is guarded on `catalog_version` still being the version it read; when the guard fails, the job asks the model again with the new lists. `src/core/catalog.ts` uses `ON CONFLICT(key) DO NOTHING` and links by key in that same batch, so names another job created are reused. Each inserted name increases `catalog_version` once; reused keys leave it alone. The guard also allows a moved version when every named key already exists. The runner distinguishes lease loss, stale inputs and database errors. Only database errors consume an attempt at publish. `jobs.stale_requeues` counts guarded stale requeues, including generation changes, up to `MAX_STALE_REQUEUES` in `src/core/job-outcome.ts`, and resets on an error retry or publish. Its neighbouring `STALE_DELAYS_MS` makes the first two stale requeues due immediately and the third and later ones due 30 seconds later. After three stale requeues, a model result bypasses the catalog-version guard and publishes by key, accepting possible near-duplicate names to avoid starvation under catalog churn. A Grok Bot Digest skips the guard at its first answer (`digestBotKind` in `src/core/digest-bot.ts` publishes with the catalog rule `unchecked`), because every requeue sends a new bot task that costs a full bot run. A near-duplicate name can result, and the weekly Tag-merge and Topic-merge Proposals can suggest merging it, which the owner accepts or rejects (`docs/proposals.md`). The Item generation and pending-value guards still apply, so a refreshed Item cannot receive an obsolete Digest. Caller-sent Digests skip the catalog guard because no model can revise them. Every publication also carries a Pin guard on the Digest's Topic key and each of its Tag keys that no stale count lifts (Catalog keys, Pins), and an accepted Tag or Topic merge bumps `catalog_version` once in its last unit, so a model Digest that read the old Tag list asks again while it has fewer than three stale requeues.
- **Catalog identity and linking.** `catalogKey` in `src/core/catalog-key.ts` writes the key described in Catalog keys, in JavaScript because SQLite lacks Unicode normalization and case folding. Unique keys keep the first spelling. `linkingDigest` replaces an Item's Topic and Tag links in the publish batch. `linkOlderDigests` (`src/core/older-digests.ts`) handles up to 10 unlinked stored Digests per sweep in Item id order with the same function, resolves each Digest's Topic name and Tag names through the Pins, writes the pinned names into the stored Digest and its `digests.topic` when a Pin changed one, and guards its batch on the stored Digest and on that resolution. The singleton `catalog_linking.last_item_id` checkpoint advances past failed rows and wraps after the final page, so corrupt rows cannot starve later Items and are retried on later passes. The Topic and Tag usage counts and unlinked scan use the indexes in `src/core/schema.ts`. A corrupt stored Digest is logged with its Item id and error and skipped. The list uses the linked Topic's name with `digests.topic` as its fallback.
- **Caller-sent Digests.** `items.pending_digest` stores the last Paste's validated Digest, or null. The fetch drops it when it stores no text; otherwise the Digest job reads its post-fetch generation and publishes under the generation and pending-value guards and the Pin guard, with every Tag name and the Topic name that a Pin covers written as the Pin's target, then clears the pending column. `startCallerTask` in `src/core/ai-tasks.ts` first creates its running AI task with executor `caller`, then publication completes it. A failed publish leaves a failed task row, as for a model result, and the sweep expires a stranded running row. Only a `running` task moves to `done` or `failed` (`completedAiTask`, `failedAiTask` and the sweep's expiry all say `state = 'running'`), so a task the sweep expired stays failed. The publishing batch also commits only while its task is still `running` (`aiTaskRunning`), so a Digest never links to a failed task: the run counts as stale input and the job requeues with a new task. After the commit, `finish` looks up the Item's open jobs to wake the runner; when that query or the wake-up fails, the failure is logged as `jobs-not-queued` and the committed work and its task stay as they are, and the sweep finds the queued job. Nullable `digests.provider` and `digests.model` preserve its provenance. The publication keeps only the `keptComments` ids that are eligible comments of the generation it publishes, once each (`eligibleAmong` in `src/core/reddit-thread.ts`, then `submittedAmong`), so the stored Digest, the task result and the Kept flags name the same comments, as for a model result. A later Paste without a Digest and Try again use the model path.

- **The weekly Proposals claim.** `openModelRun` (`src/core/proposal-week.ts`) with the kind `tag-merge` is the sweep stage `proposals`; `weeklyStages` in that file lists the five weekly stages. It claims the `scheduled_runs` row `proposals` when it is due and opens one `proposals` job in the same `db.batch`: the claim is `claimingWeek` in `src/core/scheduled-claim.ts`, and the job insert reads `changes() > 0` right after it, as `loggedTopic` does, so two ticks that race open one job and a batch that fails spends no week. Due means no row, a `last_run_at` at least 7 days old, or one more than 7 days ahead of the clock (the clock moved back). The claim holds only while a Provider is saved (`providerSet`) and at least one Item has a Digest, so with no Provider nothing is claimed and the first check runs on the first tick after one is saved. The job is owned by the Item with the highest id that has a Digest, as a Topic overview is owned by an Item of its Topic, because `jobs.item_id` is `NOT NULL` (see Deleting an Item in `data-model-outline.md` for what deleting that Item costs). A job that is still open for that owner Item blocks the insert through the open-job index, so the claim is spent and no second job opens. The handler has no `blockedBy` and takes no Topic lease, because a Tag belongs to no Topic and no Topic page changes; its deadline is `AI_DEADLINE_MS`. The tick calls no model. When the stage throws, `sweep` logs `sweep-stage-failed` and the next tick tries again, because the claim did not commit. When the job fails, one `proposals-run-failed` line names the refusal, the AI task row keeps it when a task was created (a refusal of the saved settings or the address comes before any task row), and the page shows nothing about it. The page's "last check" is the latest of the weekly claims (the Topic, Highlight and overview claims below, and the disagreement claim in `docs/proposals.md`), which does not say that a check finished.
- **The weekly Topic claim.** `openModelRun` with the kind `topic-merge` is the sweep stage `topic-proposals`, after `proposals`. It runs `claimingWeek` with that task name, so the `scheduled_runs` row, its due rule (no row, 7 days old, or more than 7 days ahead of the clock) and its Provider and Digest conditions are the Tag claim's, and it opens one `topic_proposals` job in the same `db.batch`, owned by the same newest Item with a Digest. It does not open the job in the Tag claim's batch, because `opening` decides its insert with `changes() > 0` right after its own claim: a second `opening` after the first would read the first job insert's count (0 when the open-job index blocks it) and not its claim's. A claim row of its own keeps that placement correct and makes the weekly runs independent: a failing stage or job in one does not skip the other, and a spent claim in one leaves the other's alone. Both jobs share the owner Item and have different kinds, so the open-job index allows both. The handler has no `blockedBy` and takes no Topic lease, because the check writes only `proposals` rows, and its deadline is `AI_DEADLINE_MS`. The tick calls no model. `isDue` in `src/core/jobs.ts` claims only the kinds in `JOB_KINDS`, so a release that lacks `topic_proposals` leaves a queued job unclaimed (CI, releases and rollback). Three unattended failures differ. When the stage throws before its batch commits, `sweep` logs `sweep-stage-failed` and the next tick tries again, because the claim did not commit. When the batch commits and its answer is lost (Guarded batches), the claim and the job are stored, the stage reports an error, the next tick finds the week claimed and opens nothing, and the sweep's first stage queues the stored job. When the job fails, a refusal of the saved settings or the address comes before any task row (`loadAiConfig` returns `taskId: null`), so that job leaves a `topic-proposals-run-failed` line and no `ai_tasks` row; a refusal after the task was created leaves the line and the task row with its refusal. The page shows nothing about any of them, and its "last check" is the latest of the weekly claims, which does not say a check finished.
- **The weekly Highlight claim.** `WEEKLY_RUN` (`src/core/proposal-week.ts`) maps the kind `highlight-lost` to the sweep stage `highlight-proposals`, after `topic-proposals`. It calls `proposeLostHighlights` (`src/core/highlight-proposals.ts`), which runs `plainCheckWeek` (`src/core/plain-check-week.ts`, which calls `claimingWeek` from `src/core/scheduled-claim.ts`, shared by every weekly stage) with the claim name `highlight_proposals`, so the `scheduled_runs` row, its due rule and its Provider and Digest conditions are the other claims'. The condition is shared on purpose: the Proposals page's empty state says the weekly check needs a Provider, and one rule keeps that line true, although this stage calls no Provider. The stage is one `db.batch`: the claim, the insert of up to 20 `highlight-lost` rows with `WHERE changes() > 0` right after it (the Highlights whose status is not `attached` and that have no `proposals` row in any state, newest first), and the delete of the `open` rows of this kind whose Highlight is `attached` again, placed after the insert so that the insert's `changes()` still reads the claim's row. The delete runs only when `scheduled_runs.highlight_proposals` holds this tick's time, because `changes()` after the insert counts inserted rows and cannot say whether the claim won, and a tick that did not claim the week must not delete. It opens no job and makes no AI task, because it calls no model, so it has no handler and no job kind. Two unattended failures differ. When the stage throws before its batch commits, `sweep` logs `sweep-stage-failed` and the next tick tries again, because the claim did not commit. When the batch commits and its answer is lost (Guarded batches), the rows are stored and the week is claimed, the stage reports an error, and the next tick finds the week claimed, so nothing is lost. The page shows nothing about either. `WEEKLY_TASK` (`src/core/kinds.ts`) maps each kind to its claim name, and the page's `checkedAt` is the largest `last_run_at` over those names.
- **The weekly overview claim.** `WEEKLY_RUN` (`src/core/proposal-week.ts`) maps the kind `overview-gap` to the sweep stage `overview-proposals`, after `highlight-proposals`, with the claim name `overview_proposals`. It calls `proposeOverviewGaps` (`src/core/overview-proposals.ts`), one `db.batch` of the same shape as the Highlight stage (`plainCheckWeek`): `claimingWeek`, the insert of up to 20 rows with `WHERE changes() > 0` right after it, and the delete of `open` rows whose Topic no longer has a gap, which runs only in the tick that won the claim (`claimedAt` in `src/core/scheduled-claim.ts`, the condition the Highlight delete uses too). A Topic has a gap (`overviewGap` in `src/core/topic-coverage.ts`) when `hasUncovered` holds, meaning an Item with a Digest that the Topic page's `built_from` does not list with that Digest's task id (the predicate of `notBuiltFrom`, the one the Topic page marks "Not in overview yet"; not `coverageGaps`, which also counts removed Items), and no `topic_overview` job that is not `done` is owned by any Item of the Topic (`hasOpenOverview`, the condition the Topic read calls `pending`). The insert takes the 20 Topics with the most such Items, then the lowest Topic id, that have no `proposals` row of the kind in any state, and stores them with the smaller counts first so that the Topic furthest behind gets the highest row id and lists first. It makes no job and no AI task.
- **Schedule.** One tick, every minute, claims each due task in `scheduled_runs` and enqueues jobs: the three Instance-wide weekly model runs (a `proposals` job for Tags, a `topic_proposals` job for Topics and a `disagreement_proposals` job for Items, above; none is one job for each Topic or Tag the checks touched), the two weekly checks that call no model (lost Highlights, Topics whose overview leaves out Items), which run in the sweep and open no job, one per import and Grok Bot cursor, and the sweep, which also continues every `accepting` Tag or Topic merge in its `merges` stage (`src/core/merge-accept.ts`; `docs/proposals.md`, Accept) and runs one page of the file cleanup (`src/core/tick.ts`; see Files in `files.md`). The sweep (`src/core/sweep.ts`) runs its stages one after another, the re-queue of due jobs first, so the stages after it can fail without costing a lost Queue message or an expired claim its recovery. A stage that throws is logged as `sweep-stage-failed` with its name, the later stages still run, and the sweep then throws the first error, so the tick reports it (the local scheduled endpoint answers 500). The tick calls no model, because a Cron Trigger under an hourly interval gets 30 seconds of CPU (Workers docs, Limits). Cloudflare calls the tick from a Cron Trigger; the container calls it from a timer.
